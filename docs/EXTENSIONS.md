# Scoundrel: Extension Ideas

Brainstorm doc for stretching the game past its current shape (one run, seven descents, escape). Nothing here is committed. Each section is one direction the game could grow; many of them combine. Where an idea changes the fiction, the change is kept stereotypical and no-proper-nouns, in line with the existing setting.

The current shape is the baseline. A "run" is seven descents; seven sigils opens the way out; one survivor wakes in the chamber and goes. The ideas below either widen that run, repeat it, or add lanes for replay outside it.

## 1. Other Survivors (unlockable characters)

Each run begins with someone waking in the chamber. The current game has one such someone, a blank. Extension: more than one. Each is a different *person* the dungeon could deliver, with a small permanent starting condition that bends how the entire run plays. They are not classes (no levels, no skill trees), they are starting shapes.

The point is each survivor should make the player approach the deck differently, not just nudge a stat. A survivor who only changes max HP by a few points is not pulling its weight. The lineup below tries to give each one a distinct central question.

- **The Default**: no special start. The baseline, the starting character, the shape the game was designed around.

- **The Brute**: cannot equip weapons. Diamonds drawn during a descent heal half their rank (rounded up) instead of arming. All combat resolves bare-handed. Max HP 30. The central question: how do you pace a deck when the weapon system, the spine of the base game, is gone? Every monster costs HP; potion and heart timing becomes everything.

- **The Pathfinder**: at the start of every descent, the entire deck order is visible. Cartographer-style, but for the deck, not just rooms. In exchange, the Forge offers only Transmute. Strike and Inscribe are not available. The central question: with full information and no deckbuilding, can you survive on planning alone?

- **The Collector**: keeps a satchel. At the end of every room, may store one unplayed card from that room into a satchel of size 3. Stored cards persist between rooms and between descents within a run. May spend them at any point during a fight (play a stored heart to heal mid-room, play a stored weapon when the current one is choked, play a stored monster... well, do not). Max HP 16. The central question: when do you bank, when do you spend, what is worth carrying?

- **The Tactician**: once per descent, may reorder the next 5 cards in the deck before they are drawn. Boon offers are 2 instead of 3. The central question: where do you spend your one moment of control in a 44-card descent?

- **The Mason**: the Forge opens in the *opening* sanctuary visit, before descent 1, in addition to its normal cadence (now sigils 3 and 5, two openings, not three). The central question: with the deck shapeable before the first descent, what do you build toward?

- **The Stranger**: every Boon offer is a single random Boon, take it or skip it. Two skips per run. Max HP 22. The central question: how do you play when you do not get to *choose* your tools?

How they are unlocked: each survivor unlocks through a specific in-run accomplishment, so the roster grows with play.

- The Default is unlocked from the start.
- The Brute unlocks after the player completes a full room bare-handed.
- The Pathfinder unlocks after the player completes a run that included the Cartographer Boon.
- The Collector unlocks after the player flees three rooms in a single descent with the Pickpocket Boon.
- The Tactician unlocks after the player uses the Forge in all three openings in a single run.
- The Mason unlocks after the player inscribes a custom card and wins the run.
- The Stranger unlocks after the player loses three runs in a row (a pity unlock; the character that plays for you when you cannot pick).

In fiction (within The Descent storyline): when the player dies, the chamber is empty for a while. The next time someone wakes there, it is a different person. The roster is itself a slow meta-layer: the longer you play, the more of the possible survivors you have met.

## 2. The Long Run (multiple sets of seven)

Right now the door opens at seven sigils and you walk into daylight. There is room to make the daylight a lie, or a threshold instead of an end.

### 2a. Three Acts, 21 Sigils

The full game is three sets of seven, threaded by act-transitions:

- **Act I, The Upper Halls**: the current game. Tier 1 to Tier 3 themes. Forge at sigils 2, 4, 6. At sigil 7, the high gate opens. Beyond it is not sky but a wider stairwell going down. The survivors are stunned. The dream is not where they thought it was.
- **Act II, The Deep Delvings**: themes are drawn from a Tier 2 / Tier 3 / new Tier 4 pool. New Boons appear that key off the deeper setting (cold, dark, the older things). Forge changes: Strike now accepts face-card monsters but requires *two* offerings instead of one. The sanctuary in Act II is a different chamber further down, not the one you woke in.
- **Act III, The Source**: the dungeon's voice is louder. Themes pair every descent (always two stacked). Boons are richer but rarer. The final descent is the boss-descent (see §6).

Boons and inscribed cards carry across acts within the same run. Sigils reset per act (the lock at the bottom of each act takes seven of its own). The player's deck does not reset, so by Act III the deck reflects three acts of forging.

### 2b. The False Dawn (the simpler version)

Same idea, lighter. Beat the seven and the door opens. You step out into daylight. The screen holds on the daylight for a beat. Then the daylight is wrong (too pale, too even, no sun). The player turns around and the door is gone; behind them is a new chamber and a new descent. The "real" ending is two cycles of seven (14). The second cycle's themes pull only from Tier 2 and Tier 3 and add a few Tier 4 entries.

This is cheaper than full acts and reuses everything. The single fiction beat (false dawn) does the heavy lifting.

### 2c. The Ascent

After seven descents downward, the player has to *climb back out*. The same dungeon, now changed: the themes that were assigned to descent 1 come back first, but inverted (Quiet becomes a hostile fog instead of a warm rest; Armory becomes a stripped passage with no diamonds at all). Mechanically this is a second seven-descent arc with reflected theme assignments. Fictionally the player is retracing the way they came in.

Use this if the team wants the second arc to feel like *recognition* instead of escalation. A different emotional register from the False Dawn's "the surface was never there."

### 2d. Endless

No new arc; just don't stop. After sigil 7, the door is sealed shut again and the dungeon continues. Themes escalate past Tier 3 with combined effects, Boons stop being offered, Forge stops opening, the deck never refills with new cards. The player keeps the survivors' faces they have unlocked and is now in a long death-march to see how many extra sigils they can earn. A leaderboard mode, basically.

## 3. Ascensions (difficulty tiers per character)

Borrowed shape from Slay the Spire, fitted to this game.

Each survivor (§1) has their own ladder of ascensions. Completing a run on Ascension N for a given survivor unlocks Ascension N+1 for that survivor only. The ladder layers permanent rule changes on the run:

- A1: sanctuary heals only 80% of max HP between descents.
- A2: Boon offers are 2 instead of 3.
- A3: themes escalate one tier earlier (Tier 2 enters at sigil 2).
- A4: Forge opens only twice per run.
- A5: max HP minus 2.
- A6: cannot reroll Boon offers.
- A7: every descent adds one Wound card to the deck (see §5).
- A8: face-card monsters at plus 1 effective rank.
- A9: the next descent's theme is hidden in the sanctuary.
- A10: two themes per descent from Act I onward.
- A11 through A20: continue stacking.

The point is endgame texture for players who finished the game once and want it to keep biting. The hall of the survivors becomes a long ladder per face.

## 4. Branching Descents

In the current shape, the dungeon picks the next theme and shows it. The player only chooses their Boon. Branching adds a *path* choice:

On certain sanctuary visits (say, after sigils 2, 4, 6), the player sees *two* upcoming descents side by side. Each shows its own theme and the kind of reward offered for clearing it:

- **A descent with a softer theme but only one Boon offer afterwards.**
- **A descent with a harsher theme but a guaranteed Forge opening afterwards.**
- **A descent that promises a specific named Boon in the offers (Cartographer, say) but raises the tier ceiling for the rest of the run.**

This gives the player something to *spend* on. Right now most of the choice in a run is in Boons and Forge. Branching descents add a strategy layer at the macro level: which fork is worth the cost.

Fictionally: more than one stair leads down from the sanctuary. Some passages are colder, some go past abandoned rooms, some go straight to the deeper places. The player picks which way to descend tonight.

## 5. New Card Types

The base game uses the 44-card Scoundrel deck (no red face cards, no jokers). Extensions could add categories that themes, Boons, or the Forge introduce.

- **Wound cards**: enter the deck when the player takes damage past a threshold in a descent. Drawn like a monster but does nothing; just clogs the room. Could be removed at the Forge for free (no offering needed). Themes like Iron Bones could load extra Wounds.
- **Sigil-cards**: rare, only seeded by specific Boons. When drawn and survived past, give a one-shot benefit (heal 4, refresh weapon, see next 3 cards).
- **Echo cards**: blank cards that copy the previous card played in the same room. Already proposed as Mirror Shard in the Forge frames. Could become a full card type if Themes like Echo (Tier 3) want them.
- **Ash cards**: a fifth "suit" (visually marked, mechanically distinct) introduced by the deepest themes. Ash cards count as none of the four normal suits, ignore Boons that key off suit, and pass through weapons.

Pick at most one of these and make it work; do not pile them on at once.

## 6. Bosses (the deferred section made real)

The current design defers bosses (§10 of DESIGN.md). A few shapes they could take inside the loop:

### 6a. The Boss-Descent

At sigil 7 (or at the end of each Act in §2a), the descent is a *single special card* instead of a deck. The card has a multi-stage HP bar and its own rule mutation that supersedes the theme. The player walks in with their full Boon and deck state, plays the fight as a series of decisions (attack with weapon, attack bare, drink potion, flee a phase), and either wins the sigil or dies.

This breaks the "one descent equals one Scoundrel game" rule on purpose: boss descents are the only descents that do.

### 6b. The Stalker

A persistent card seeded into every descent from sigil 4 onward. Always the same card (visually distinct), always at the same point in the deck (say, fifth from the top). If the player kills it, it returns to the deck next descent at plus 1 rank. The Stalker is not a boss you fight once; it is a presence you manage. Killing it three times across descents is the win condition for an alternate "ending."

### 6c. The Named Three

One boss per Act, three total in the §2a structure. Each boss has a hidden "name" (a face-card rank, in mechanical terms). Naming the boss before fighting it (Forge: Strike + Inscribe combo on the matching face-card) weakens its rank by 2. This ties Forge directly to the boss layer.

## 7. Meta-Progression Beyond the Run

The current meta layer (§7 of DESIGN.md) is deliberately light: three Memory Slots, a Codex of winning inscribed cards, daily seed. Extensions could add a small handful of persistent layers without bloating into a stat-stick grind.

- **The Roster**: see §1. Each survivor unlocked is one more face on the run-start screen. Shows unlock conditions for the ones still locked.
- **The Tally**: every Strike performed in a *winning* run is recorded. Players see a running count of which card ranks/suits they have removed from the deck across all winning runs. No mechanical effect; statistical reward.
- **The Forge Frames Codex**: new custom-card frames unlock through play. Lucky Coin is in from the start. Black Pact unlocks after surviving three Tier 3 descents in a single run. Skeleton Key unlocks after a Pacifist run (see §9). Etc.
- **Theme Almanac**: themes encountered are logged. Encountering all Tier 1 themes unlocks a "Choose your theme" mode for descent 1 of future runs.
- **Boon Library**: a Boon does not appear in offers until first encountered in a run. The player builds up the pool of possible Boons over many runs by surviving descents where new ones are added. Slow-burn.

None of these grant raw power. They unlock breadth.

## 8. Daily and Weekly Seeds

Already proposed (§7 DESIGN.md). Worth expanding:

- **Daily Coffin**: a fixed seed where everyone plays the same survivor, same Boon offers, same theme sequence, same Forge offers. Leaderboard by final HP at sigil 7. Optional sharing of the seed string for friends to attempt.
- **Weekly Pilgrimage**: a longer seed, two-cycle (False Dawn), with a custom rule layered on (e.g. "no Forge"). Resets weekly.
- **The Mirror Run**: a seed where two players (asynchronous) get the same deck and theme sequence but different Boon offers. Compare runs.

These are pure replayability surfaces; they cost mostly UI work.

## 9. Alternate Modes

Self-contained game modes, each picked from the run-start screen. They use the same systems but tilt one rule hard.

- **Pacifist**: complete descents without slaying any monster above rank 6. Monsters above 6 must be fled or potion-skipped (potions could be re-skinned as bribes for this mode). Sigils only awarded for descents that stay under the rank cap.
- **Hardcore**: no Boons, no Forge. Just deck and weapon. Win condition stays at seven sigils. Pure baseline test.
- **The Ledger**: every action you take is "paid for" in deck cost. Drinking a potion adds a Wound; using the Forge removes one Boon offer next sanctuary. A mode for players who want a tighter, more accountant-flavored run.
- **Two-Survivor Tag**: pick two survivors at the run start. Alternate which one wakes each sanctuary visit. Each carries their own Boons (Boons earned by one do not benefit the other). Two HP pools.
- **The Quiet Run**: every descent runs under The Quiet. The dungeon is asleep the whole way down. Themes do not exist. A no-pressure mode for new players or a way to just enjoy the deck mechanics.

## 10. Setting Variants (different fictions, same rules)

The systems are setting-neutral. That opens the door to *swappable settings*: the same game, with different card art and theme names and sanctuary art, themed differently. The Descent (the current shape: chamber, dungeon, daylight) is one setting among many.

- **The Drowned Monastery**: a flooded cliff-side monastery, the monks gone, the things in the lower cells were never men. Sanctuary is the chapel above the tide line. The lock is the bell that has to be rung seven times.
- **The Buried Garden**: a noble estate sunk into the earth in a forgotten cataclysm. The dead are the household: servants, hunters, lords. Sanctuary is the conservatory. The lock is the front gate, choked with vines.
- **The Hollow Ship**: a ship that did not sink, just stopped. Decks below the waterline are crawling. Sanctuary is the captain's cabin. The lock is the wheel, which only turns once all seven compass-stones are returned to the binnacle.
- **The Quiet Town**: a village whose main street ends at a stone stairway going down. Sanctuary is the inn at the top of the stair. The lock is a chain of seven bells, one per descent.

Each setting reskins theme names, Boon names, monster art, sanctuary art. The systems are identical. The Forge offerings have setting-appropriate wording. Players unlock settings by completing runs in earlier ones.

This is the cheapest large-feeling extension because the engine work is zero. The cost is art and writing.

## 11. Other Loose Ideas

A grab-bag of smaller things that do not yet fit into one of the above.

- **Companion cards**: a Boon adds a single neutral card to the deck that follows you between descents. Drawing it triggers a small effect (heal 2, peek next card). It is "yours."
- **The Vow**: at the start of a descent, the player can swear a vow ("no potions," "no fleeing," "weapon only"). Honoring the vow grants a guaranteed Boon offer of the player's choice afterwards. Breaking the vow forfeits the next Boon offer.
- **The Shrine**: a one-time sanctuary feature unlocked after a specific accomplishment. Lets the player trade one Boon for two new offers. Costs a sigil.
- **Inherited Goods**: when a run ends in death, one specific item (the last weapon equipped, say) is left in the chamber for the next survivor to find. Tiny meta-progression hook, fits the fiction.
- **Two-Lock Doors**: rare descent variant where the descent ends with two unlocked exits. One leads back to sanctuary as normal; the other leads to a bonus sub-descent (5 cards, no theme, one Boon at the end). High risk, high reward.
- **The Quiet, Again**: a Boon that lets the player force The Quiet onto one descent of their choice, once per run. Currently The Quiet only appears on descent 1.

## 12. What to Try First

Cheapest big wins first, in rough order:

1. **The False Dawn** (§2b). Two cycles of seven, single fiction beat at the midpoint. Almost no new systems, doubles the run length, gives players the "is this the end?" moment.
2. **Other Survivors** (§1) with just two or three faces to start (The Default, The Brute, The Tactician). Each is a small constant injected at run start. Tiny system change, large felt change.
3. **Ascensions** (§3) for the unlocked survivors. Pure tuning knobs reused as a ladder. No new content needed.
4. **Branching Descents** (§4) at one sanctuary visit (after sigil 4). Test the strategy layer before committing to three branch points.
5. **Bosses, Stalker variant** (§6b). Cheapest of the three boss shapes; reuses existing card rendering.

The Three Acts (§2a), Ascent (§2c), and Setting Variants (§10) are bigger lifts and should wait until at least one of the above has shipped and held player attention.
